## 2. Correct the λ_H input before extending it

For a simple system at fixed composition,

\[
dH=T\,dS+V\,dP,\qquad \lambda_H=H/S.
\]

On an S > 0 chart choose a fixed reference S₀ and write Σ = ln(S/S₀). Then direct differentiation gives

\[
\boxed{d\lambda_H=\frac{V}{S}\,dP+(T-\lambda_H)\,d\Sigma.}
\]

Thus the coefficient of dΣ contains T, not T/S. The older draft gives the correct expression in one section but uses τ = T/S in another. Both formulas cannot describe this same potential and this same Σ. This is an algebraic normalization correction, not an extra physical assumption.

The actual affine-coordinate Hessian entries are

\[
\begin{aligned}
g_{PP}&=-\frac{V}{S K_S},\\
g_{P\Sigma}&=\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial S}\right)_P-\frac{V}{S}
=\left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial P}\right)_S-\frac{V}{S},\\
g_{\Sigma\Sigma}&=\frac{ST}{C_P}-T+\lambda_H,
\end{aligned}
\]

where K_S = −V(∂P/∂V)_S and C_P = T(∂S/∂T)_P. The last equation follows from ∂Σ T = ST/C_P and ∂Σ λ_H = T − λ_H.

For V, S, K_S > 0, g_PP < 0. Consequently this raw H/S Hessian is not a positive Riemannian metric on the full (P, Σ) chart. Its nondegeneracy and full signature still have to be checked. Nondegeneracy permits the pseudo-Kähler construction below. A positive Kähler sector requires a justified convex potential or a separately derived positive structure. Multiplying the potential by −1 does not repair a mixed signature, and squaring coefficients would define a different metric.

There is also a coordinate requirement: ∂a∂b λ transforms as a tensor under affine coordinate changes, not arbitrary coordinate changes. Specify a torsion-free flat affine connection ∇⁰ and use g = ∇⁰dλ. In its affine coordinates this is the displayed Hessian. It is not the Hessian formed using the Levi–Civita connection of g.

Finally, the exact equilibrium response one-form dλ_H has zero exterior derivative. It cannot itself supply a nondegenerate Kähler two-form by taking d(dλ_H). The two-form constructed in §4 lives on the lifted space and is a different object.

## 3. The native complex sector and its transport obstruction

Work locally over real smooth scalar coefficients with the R1/R2 algebra

\[
\mathcal A=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{I,R,K,L\},\quad
R^2=-I,\quad K^2=I,\quad KR=-RK,\quad L=KR.
\]

Then L² = I and L† = L. This algebraic discussion precedes a metric or pairing.

### Proposition 1: a complex subalgebra is present

The subalgebra C_R = span{I,R} has multiplication

\[
(aI+bR)(cI+dR)=(ac-bd)I+(ad+bc)R.
\]

With real coefficients it is isomorphic to the ordinary complex field, with R realizing i. With coefficients in a different cut field, the corresponding quadratic extension must be checked over that field; the real smooth chart is an explicit realization here.

The larger algebra has additional noncommuting directions K and L. This gives a concrete algebraic meaning to a structure richer than a complex scalar. It does not by itself establish greater physical fundamentality.

### Proposition 2: exact transport compatibility

In a local frame with constant R, take an algebra-valued connection

\[
A_a=\alpha_aI+\beta_aR+\gamma_aK+\delta_aL.
\]

Using the defining relations,

\[
[K,R]=2L,\qquad [L,R]=-2K,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{D_aR=\partial_aR+[A_a,R]
=2\gamma_aL-2\delta_aK.}
\]

Since K and L are linearly independent,

\[
\boxed{D_aR=0\iff\gamma_a=\delta_a=0.}
\]

Thus the centralizer of R is precisely C_R. Parallel transport preserves the selected complex structure exactly when its local coefficients lie in this centralizer. If R varies with position the invariant condition remains D_aR = 0, and ∂aR must be included.

This is a concrete native compatibility condition. It is not yet a Kähler theorem: the algebra must act on a tangent carrier, its R action must be integrable, a compatible metric must be supplied, and the relevant connection must be related to Levi–Civita transport. Nor does imposing γ = δ = 0 automatically define a consistent truncation of an unknown parent dynamics. Its discarded field equations must also vanish on the proposed sector.

### Two cautions about projection

1. There is no unital real-algebra homomorphism from this entire R/K algebra into the commutative field C. If such a map existed, anticommutation would give 2π(K)π(R) = 0, while π(K) and π(R) would both be invertible by their squared relations. This is impossible. Selecting C_R is a subalgebra restriction; it is not a faithful scalar quotient of the full algebra.
2. A homomorphism from the seam algebra into a commutative field must send p = I − ST to zero, since TS = I makes both scalar images inverses. Such a scalar shadow erases the nonzero aperture p. Retaining that memory requires a richer carrier or readout than a scalar field homomorphism.

### What becomes of g + iΩ?

For real scalar coefficients and an antisymmetric Ω, the native expression

\[
\mathscr H_{AB}=\mathcal G_{AB}I+R\Omega_{AB}
\]

satisfies \(\mathscr H_{BA}^{\dagger}=\mathscr H_{AB}\), using R† = −R. Additional terms K P_AB + L Q_AB obey the same dagger symmetry when P and Q are real symmetric. These are algebraic compatibility statements; positivity and the Kähler differential conditions do not follow from the notation.

For example g = I₂ and Ω = [[0,2],[-2,0]] give (g⁻¹Ω)² = −4I₂, not −I₂. Symmetry plus antisymmetry alone does not select a complex structure.

### Theorem 3

The triple (𝒢,J,Ω) is pseudo-Kähler. It is Kähler if g is positive definite.

**Proof.** First J² = −I and J has constant components in these affine coordinates, so its Nijenhuis tensor vanishes. Direct evaluation gives 𝒢(JX,JY) = 𝒢(X,Y) and Ω(X,Y) = 𝒢(JX,Y). The form Ω is nondegenerate because g is. Moreover

\[

because λ_abc is symmetric in c,a. These are the integrable Hermitian/pseudo-Hermitian and closed-form conditions. Equivalently the Levi–Civita connection preserves J. ∎

The affine transition rule (x,y) ↦ (Bx+b,By) preserves this construction. A global conclusion requires the corresponding globally specified Hessian manifold and compatible transition data; an arbitrary list of Hessians in unrelated charts is insufficient.

Since J† = −J and K̃† = K̃ relative to 𝒢, the retrieved native dagger is respected in this realization.

With zᵃ = xᵃ + iyᵃ, choose the complex-coordinate convention

## 5. The exact λ equation for an Einstein sector

For this metric the Ricci components are

\[
\operatorname{Ric}_{a\bar b}
=-\partial_a\partial_{\bar b}\log|\det h|
=-\frac14\partial_{x^a}\partial_{x^b}\log|\det D^2\lambda|.
\]

The absolute value permits a nondegenerate indefinite h; its determinant has a fixed sign on the chosen connected chart. The formula follows by tracing the Kähler connection \(\Gamma^c_{ab}=h^{c\bar d}\partial_a h_{b\bar d}\), using \(\Gamma^c_{ac}=\partial_a\log|\det h|\), and differentiating with −∂̄b. The factor 1/4 follows from ∂z = (∂x − i∂y)/2 and y independence.

### Theorem 4: determinant–Einstein equivalence

For a fixed real constant c,

\[
\operatorname{Ric}(\mathcal G)=c\mathcal G
\]

if and only if

\[
\partial_a\partial_b\left(\log|\det D^2\lambda|+4c\lambda\right)=0.
\]

On the connected affine chart this is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{|\det D^2\lambda|=\exp(a_i x^i+b-4c\lambda).}
\]

**Proof.** Substitute h = D²λ into the Ricci formula and move c h to the left. A twice differentiable function with zero Hessian is affine on a connected open chart. Reverse the calculation for the converse. The remaining complex-type components of both tensors vanish, so the equality is the full real Einstein condition. ∎

For positive g the absolute value can be removed. For indefinite g, det D²λ = ε exp(a·x+b−4cλ) with fixed ε ∈ {−1,+1}. Reference units make the determinant ratio inside the logarithm dimensionless; the constant b absorbs a fixed determinant normalization. The constants a,b are chartwise integration data, not newly predicted physical constants.

In real dimension d = 2n ≥ 4 this implies

\[
\operatorname{Ric}-\tfrac12 R\mathcal G+\Lambda_{\rm geom}\mathcal G=0,
\qquad \Lambda_{\rm geom}=(n-1)c.
\]

This is an Einstein metric in the state-space lift. A four-dimensional lift can have positive, negative or neutral even signature; it is not thereby a physical Lorentzian spacetime.

### A controlled sourced extension

If one separately specifies an Einstein-type source equation in the same y-independent, J-compatible tensor sector,

\[
\operatorname{Ric}_{a\bar b}=c h_{a\bar b}+\mathcal Q_{a\bar b},
\]

with real symmetric Q_ab in this chart, its λ form is

\[
\boxed{\partial_a\partial_b(\log|\det D^2\lambda|+4c\lambda)
=-4\mathcal Q_{ab}.}
\]

It imposes local integrability conditions ∂c Q_ab = ∂a Q_cb. If Q_ab = ∂a∂b q locally, the integrated equation is

\[
|\det D^2\lambda|=\exp(a_i x^i+b-4c\lambda-4q).
\]

This is an exact rewriting of a specified compatible source law. It is not a derivation of arbitrary stress-energy, nor a universal noncommutative UGD determinant equation. A general source may fail the required symmetry, type, y-independence, conservation or integrability conditions.

### Two checks that separate construction from dynamics

* λ = |x|²/2 gives constant h, zero Ricci curvature and c = 0.
* λ = −Σ_i log xⁱ on xⁱ > 0 gives h_ab = δ_ab/(xᵃ)². Here log det h = 2λ and c = −1/2. The metric is a product of negatively curved two-dimensional factors with the same Einstein constant. For n ≥ 2 it is Einstein and nonflat, with mixed-factor sectional curvatures zero, so the theorem has nontrivial solutions.

For example the coupled polynomial tested in the appendix has h = I at the origin while its real Ricci x block is diag(1,0), so Ricci cannot be a scalar multiple of the metric there.

## 7. What is required to turn this into a parent λ/UGD field theory?

### The signature interface

A real metric compatible with an orthogonal complex structure has even positive and even negative signature indices. To see this, a non-null X is orthogonal to JX and has the same norm: G(X,JX) = 0 and G(JX,JX) = G(X,X). Decompose into J-invariant nondegenerate two-planes and iterate.

Thus a four-dimensional pseudo-Kähler metric may have signature (4,0), (2,2) or (0,4), but not (3,1). The lift of a base Hessian of signature (p,q) has signature (2p,2q). A physical Lorentzian metric must therefore arise through an additional specified realization, reduction or propagation construction. Calling the companion y coordinate time does not supply that construction.

### The variational interface

Suppose a physical metric is constrained to q_ab = ∂a∂b λ in fixed affine coordinates. Define the metric Euler density by

\[
\delta S=\int\sqrt{|q|}\,E^{ab}\delta q_{ab}\,d^dx.
\]

Restricting to δq_ab = ∂a∂b δλ and integrating by parts twice gives only

\[
\boxed{\frac{\delta S}{\delta\lambda}
=\partial_a\partial_b\big(\sqrt{|q|}E^{ab}\big)=0.}
\]

It does not generally imply E^{ab} = 0: a double-divergence operator has a nontrivial kernel. Einstein solutions satisfy the restricted equation, but equivalence is not established by that fact.

For a more general native-to-metric map q = Q[Λ, memory, connection, …], the equation is the formal adjoint of its linearized map acting on the metric Euler expression, with further native field equations. A genuine Einstein-reduction theorem must establish that this adjoint is injective on the allowed Euler expressions (modulo the actual gauge identities), or supply sufficiently complete independent variations and constraints. Observational target-faithfulness alone is not a proof of this variational property.

### A concrete next native development

Use the full native connection and an explicitly typed module-valued potential Λ. In an affine coordinate frame, the second response splits exactly as

\[
D_aD_b\boldsymbol\Lambda
=\tfrac12(D_aD_b+D_bD_a)\boldsymbol\Lambda
+\tfrac12[D_a,D_b]\boldsymbol\Lambda.
\]

For a left-module connection, [D_a,D_b]Λ = F_abΛ; in the adjoint representation it is [F_ab,Λ]. These are different representations and must not be interchanged. For an ordinary scalar acted on only by commuting partial derivatives, this antisymmetric part vanishes identically.

This specifies where a genuine noncommuting response can enter. To obtain a field equation one must still derive/select a native variational or balance law, its invariant pairing/trace, admissible variations, and its spacetime realization. The correct complex-sector gate is D R = 0; the correct physical Einstein gate is the consistent action/variation reduction. Setting unexplained “UGD corrections” to zero in a displayed Einstein equation would only label the desired result.
