\begin{abstract}
We present a unified theorem chain for cut-graded recognition dynamics and test its finite adapters on a frozen nuclear binding-energy dataset. The mathematical core is organized as five numbered results. Theorem 41 gives the unique cut-even/cut-odd decomposition of a generator, bilateral exponential reconstruction, an operator cut-square identity, cut-loop memory and seam curvature, periodic and antiperiodic closure sectors, a clock-free fixed-space projector, and faithful component observation. Theorem 42 lifts a response section to a typed covariant derivative tower, propagates the cut grading through every derivative order, decomposes the first Jacobian jointly by cut parity and transpose parity, and proves monotone reduction of target-relevant recognition kernels. Theorem 43 combines the generator and tower into a bilateral jet-flow capstone with exact finite-order reconstruction, an integral remainder, alternating jet parity, first-recognition order, decoder burden, and fail-closed classification. Theorem 44 supplies a Madhava--Smriti corrected-recursion theorem: a finite jet body, a lawful correction window, and a typed tail satisfy an exact closure identity, refinement transfers one jet term from tail to body without changing the recognized limit, and the tail itself inherits bilateral cut parity.

Two distinct cut-square identities are then separated. The operator identity
\[
  (U_t+U_{-t})^2-(U_t-U_{-t})^2=4I
\]
expresses bilateral flow reconstruction for a cut-odd generator. The positive-metric identity
\[
 \|c\|_H^2\|y\|_H^2-|\langle c,y\rangle_H|^2
 =\|H^{1/2}c\wedge H^{1/2}y\|^2
\]
expresses decoder coherence and transverse response residue. They are compatible but not interchangeable.

Theorem 45 is the frozen nuclear adapter and validation theorem. On an AME/NUBASE-derived chart of 3,558 evaluated nuclei, the bilateral and Madhava--Smriti wiring identities pass for every evaluated case: the maximum bilateral reconstruction defect is $1.8189894035458565\times10^{-12}$ keV/A, while the validation-closure and correction-to-tail transfer defects are exactly zero in the generated report. The refinement audit classifies 2,578 nuclei as contractive-memory and 980 as open-refinement. A guarded decoder-selection rule, preserving 3,514 predictions and 44 abstentions, reduces mean absolute residual from 10.2147 to 7.5174 keV/A and root mean square residual from 54.7503 to 29.3292 keV/A relative to the frozen two-axis baseline at identical coverage. These are reproducible internal and cross-fitted validation results. They do not identify a fundamental nuclear Hamiltonian, prove a universal nuclear law, or constitute temporally future-blind external validation.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Recognition Kernel Framework; cut-graded generator; bilateral flow; covariant response jets; Madhava--Smriti closure; cut-square identity; decoder burden; nuclear binding energy; guarded prediction; abstention.
