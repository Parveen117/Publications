\begin{abstract}
We develop a cut-graded recognition architecture and test it in two finite physical domains. The mathematical core has a dependency hierarchy rather than four independent layers of novelty. Theorem 41 gives the unique cut-even/cut-odd decomposition of a bounded generator, bilateral flow conjugacy, an operator cut-square identity, cut-loop memory and commutator curvature, closure sectors, a clock-free fixed-space projector, and faithful component observation. Theorem 42 propagates a declared cut through a typed covariant response tower and identifies target-relevant blind directions. Theorem 43 is a capstone synthesis that adds finite observed-flow reconstruction, an integral remainder, first-recognition order, and decoder burden. Theorem 44 records a typed refinement ledger: adding one response jet transfers one fixed term from the tail to the finite body while preserving the recognized state. Classical ingredients, including involutive gradings, Taylor remainders, Lagrange's identity, and range factorization, are identified explicitly; the claimed contribution is their fail-closed integration and validated adapter design.

Two non-interchangeable cut-square identities are separated. The operator identity
\[
  (U_t+U_{-t})^2-(U_t-U_{-t})^2=4I
\]
expresses bilateral reconstruction for a cut-odd generator, whereas
\[
 \|c\|_H^2\|y\|_H^2-|\langle c,y\rangle_H|^2
 =\|H^{1/2}c\wedge H^{1/2}y\|^2
\]
is a positive-metric decomposition of decoder-aligned and transverse response energy.

In the nuclear adapter, the cut exchanges neutron- and proton-direction response packets. On an AME/NUBASE-derived chart of 3,558 evaluated nuclei, exact zero closure and correction-transfer defects are treated as implementation checks, while the empirical result is a guarded decoder-selection rule that preserves 3,514 predictions and 44 abstentions and reduces mean absolute residual from 10.2147 to 7.5174 keV/A and root mean square residual from 54.7503 to 29.3292 keV/A relative to the frozen two-axis baseline at identical coverage. A separate electronic adapter conditionally derives the 118-position period/block skeleton from Pauli counting and declared Madelung order, then audits frozen NIST ground configurations for H--U: 75 of 92 match the skeleton exactly and 17 carry finite charge-conserving donor--acceptor residues. These are reproducible internal and source-bounded validations. They do not identify a universal physical generator, derive Madelung order from the many-electron Hamiltonian, or constitute future-blind external validation.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Recognition Kernel Framework; involutive grading; bilateral flow; covariant response jets; typed remainder; cut-square identity; decoder burden; nuclear binding energy; periodic-table skeleton; guarded prediction; abstention.
