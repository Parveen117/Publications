\section{Conclusion}

A cut becomes mathematically useful only when it grades a declared carrier, a generator becomes physically useful only when its observer and target are typed, and a residual becomes memory only when its ledger and transport law are fixed before evaluation. Theorem 41 supplies the cut-graded generator and bilateral algebra. Theorem 42 propagates the structure through a covariant response tower. Theorem 43 turns finite jets into a controlled observed flow with target-relative recognition order and burden. Theorem 44 makes correction and tail one exact refinement ledger. The positive-metric cut-square theorem separates decoder-aligned response from transverse mismatch. Theorem 45 shows that these interfaces can be wired into a reproducible nuclear validation pipeline and can improve a guarded prediction rule at unchanged coverage while preserving abstention and negative controls.

The result is deliberately less grandiose than a universal nuclear theory and more useful than one: it is a theorem-governed recognition architecture with exact finite certificates, a large frozen validation chart, explicit failures, source pins, and a clear next experiment. The decisive remaining step is temporal or independently held-out validation after the complete decoder and guard structure are frozen.
