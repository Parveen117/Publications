\section{Synthesis and interpretation}

\subsection{What the theorem chain achieves}

The source-numbered results form one dependency route rather than four parallel discoveries:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{cut and generator}
 \to \text{even/odd grading}
 \to \text{bilateral flow and operator cut square},\\
&\text{typed response tower}
 \to \text{nested recognition kernels}
 \to \text{finite reconstruction},\\
&\text{finite reconstruction}
 \to \text{typed refinement ledger}
 \to \text{memory-closed or fail-closed classification}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The nuclear and electronic sections show that the route can be consumed by two distinct finite carriers without erasing negative controls. The nuclear adapter uses neutron/proton directional exchange and a held-out prediction task. The electronic adapter uses electron--hole or donor--acceptor exchange and a source-bounded configuration audit.

\subsection{Standard ingredients and claimed contribution}

The individual ingredients have classical neighbours: involutive gradings, operator exponentials, Taylor remainders, covariant jets, Lagrange's identity, frame or range bounds, and selective prediction. The paper does not claim these objects were unknown. Its proposed contribution is the exact governance interface between them:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item a declared involution grades the generator, observed jets, and residual channels;
\item bilateral flow supplies an internal covariance test before prediction;
\item recognition kernels identify the first derivative order at which a target becomes visible;
\item decoder burden separates visibility from quantitatively admissible recovery;
\item refinement transfers a declared term from tail to body without silently discarding the earlier state;
\item the metric cut square resolves decoder-aligned and transverse response energy;
\item source and abstention boundaries remain part of the theorem ledger;
\item the same abstract governance supports two physically different exchange maps without asserting one universal physical Hamiltonian.
\end{enumerate}

The framework names are retained for traceability and conceptual compression, but the standard mathematical meaning is given first wherever a name could otherwise obscure the carrier.

\subsection{What the physical cuts do and do not mean}

In the nuclear adapter,
\[
 J(B_N,B_Z)=(B_Z,B_N)
\]
exchanges neutron- and proton-direction response packets. Its odd coordinate is directional disagreement. In the electronic skeleton,
\[
 J_c(q)=c-q
\]
exchanges electron and hole occupancy within one subshell; in the ground-state residue ledger, $J_{a,b}$ exchanges the active donor and acceptor channels. These are explicit physical response-space involutions.

They do not yet establish that a unique cut acts on the full nuclear or many-electron state space. The abstract theorem requires a declared involution; the adapters show two lawful choices and test their consequences. Derivation of either involution from a microscopic Hamiltonian remains open.

\subsection{Why the negative results are load-bearing}

Several attempted promotions fail, and those failures sharpen the theory:
\begin{itemize}
\item a simple aggregate of directional observers can cancel even when direct-sum energy is nonzero;
\item adding jet depth does not guarantee monotone empirical error reduction for every nucleus;
\item the V6 fallback and order-selected point predictors do not outperform V5.1;
\item the strict V6 MAE improvement is caused by additional abstention, not new retained values;
\item the correction ratio has effectively zero correlation with the held-out error in the stressed sector;
\item a one-step fixed-space test can contain stroboscopic nonzero modes absent from the clock-free Eye;
\item only seven of the seventeen H--U promotion residues terminate at an exact half-filled or full inner subshell, so the classroom slogan does not explain the full ledger;
\item electronic configurations for $Z=93,\ldots,118$ are abstained because the frozen source ends at uranium.
\end{itemize}
A framework that records only successful examples is not a recognition system; it is a scrapbook.

\subsection{Comparison obligations}

The current common-support FRDM(2012) table compares FRDM with frozen UAM-V4, not with Recognition Repair. A valid ranking of V5.1 against FRDM, WS4, Duflo--Zuker, or machine-learning models requires prediction-level data on identical nuclei, identical target units, explicit treatment of estimated masses, and a frozen comparison script. Until that run exists, the paper reports the available UAM-V4 comparison and labels the direct V5.1 benchmark open.

Likewise, the 75/92 electronic match count is a frozen occupancy audit, not a predictive promotion model. A stronger electronic test must hold out periods or blocks and predict whether a configuration has nonzero residue without reading its NIST label.

\subsection{Unbounded and continuum extensions}

The bounded theory extends to an unbounded generator only after domain invariance, closability, and exponential generation are proved. A future theorem should specify a common core $\mathcal D$, establish $J\Dom(G)=\Dom(G)$, control $[\Ge,\Go]$ on the core or in form sense, and prove that the observed jet series converges in the chosen topology. None of these obligations is discharged by a finite nilpotent certificate.

Likewise, a continuum nuclear response bundle would require a declared state manifold or graph, a connection, physically typed response fibres, and an adapter from measured mass-table coordinates to the abstract cut and observer. The present finite validation is a useful constraint on such an adapter, not its derivation. A microscopic electronic extension must derive or replace the declared Madelung order using a many-electron energy principle and must handle relativistic and correlation effects with source-typed uncertainty.
