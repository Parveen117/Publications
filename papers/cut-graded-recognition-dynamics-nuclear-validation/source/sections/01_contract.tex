\section{Reader contract, source discipline, and theorem map}

This paper assembles theorem capsules developed in the public Recognition Kernel Framework repository with deterministic adapters developed in the public Atomic Validation repository \cite{rkfRelease,atomicRelease}. The manuscript is not a retrospective relabelling of numerical observations. Its audit order is
\[
\text{native space}\to\text{cut}\to\text{generator}\to\text{flow}\to
\text{observer}\to\text{target}\to\text{residue}\to\text{validation}.
\]
A later object is never used to redefine an earlier datum after the residual has been inspected.

\subsection{The five-result architecture}

The paper preserves the development numbering used by the source programme.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.6em,style=nextline]
\item[Theorem 41.] Certified cut-graded universal generator theorem.
\item[Theorem 42.] Cut-graded lambda-Jacobian tower and thermodynamic recognition theorem.
\item[Theorem 43.] Bilateral jet-flow recognition capstone theorem.
\item[Theorem 44.] Madhava--Smriti bilateral jet-flow closure theorem.
\item[Theorem 45.] Frozen nuclear recognition adapter and validation theorem.
\end{description}

Theorem 45 is distinguished from the first four results. Theorems 41--44 are operator and response-theoretic statements. Theorem 45 is a deterministic validation theorem on a fixed dataset, fixed code path, fixed guards, and fixed report artefacts. Calling both kinds of statement ``theorems'' does not erase their different hypotheses.

\subsection{Three levels of evidence}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{E\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.7em]
\item \textbf{Formal proof.} Operator identities are proved from stated Hilbert-space, cut, domain, and boundedness hypotheses.
\item \textbf{Exact finite certificate.} Rational fixtures verify theorem wiring, negative controls, and finite-rank consequences without floating-point fitting.
\item \textbf{Empirical validation.} Frozen nuclear data test the adapter, guards, coverage, residuals, subgroup behaviour, and decoder selection.
\end{enumerate}

An exact certificate is not empirical evidence for a physical identification. An empirical improvement is not a proof of an operator theorem. Their value lies in meeting at a typed interface.

\subsection{Claim discipline}

The paper does not claim that one physical generator has been derived for all systems, that the algebraic cut is uniquely nuclear, that every residual is memory, or that a cross-fitted historical validation is a future prediction. The source data support a narrower result: a cut-graded recognition architecture has exact finite realizations and a guarded nuclear adapter whose declared identities and performance metrics are reproducible.

\section{Native notation and admissibility hypotheses}

Let $\Hh$ and $\Yy$ be complex Hilbert spaces. Let
\[
 J=J^*=J^{-1}
\]
be a self-adjoint unitary involution, called the \emph{cut}. Its spectral projections are
\[
 P_\pm=\frac{I\pm J}{2}.
\]
Let $G$ be a bounded operator on $\Hh$ unless an unbounded extension is explicitly invoked. Define
\[
 U_t=e^{tG}.
\]
Let $B:\Hh\to\Yy$ be a bounded observer and let $L:\Hh\to\C$ be a scalar target.

For an unbounded $G$, every displayed identity involving $JGJ$, $e^{tG}$, commutators, or powers of $G$ is licensed only after a common invariant core $\mathcal D$ is specified such that
\[
 J\mathcal D=\mathcal D,
 \qquad
 G\mathcal D\subseteq\mathcal D,
\]
and the required closures or functional calculi exist. This manuscript proves the bounded theory and records the unbounded interface as an open extension unless the stated analytic hypotheses are supplied.

\begin{principle}[Fail-closed ordering]
Algebraic covariance, carrier typing, and recognition-kernel admissibility are checked before a predictive value is promoted. A failed structural gate produces an open-seam or abstention state, not an opportunity to rename the defect after observing the target.
\end{principle}
