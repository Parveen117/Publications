\section{Reader contract, core idea, and source discipline}

This paper assembles theorem capsules developed in the public Recognition Kernel Framework repository with deterministic adapters developed in the public Atomic Validation repository \cite{rkfRelease,atomicRelease}. The manuscript is not a retrospective relabelling of numerical observations. Its audit order is
\[
\text{native space}\to\text{cut}\to\text{generator}\to\text{flow}\to
\text{observer}\to\text{target}\to\text{residue}\to\text{validation}.
\]
A later object is never used to redefine an earlier datum after the residual has been inspected.

\subsection{Core idea in plain language}

The construction begins with a system that has two declared sectors. A self-adjoint involution $J$ labels those sectors. Every generator $G$ then splits uniquely into an even part that acts within the sectors and an odd part that transports between them. When the generator is purely odd, forward and backward evolution are related by conjugation with $J$.

Measurements of the evolving system are organized by derivative order. The zeroth-order measurement records the observed value, the first records its first response, and higher orders record progressively finer response information. Stacking these measurements can shrink the set of states invisible to the observer. For a declared target, the first derivative order that removes every target-relevant blind direction is its first recognition order.

A finite derivative expansion leaves a tail. The paper records that tail in the same typed carrier as the observed flow and tracks exactly how one additional derivative term moves from the tail into the finite approximation. In the nuclear adapter, the two sectors are the neutron-direction and proton-direction response channels. Their average is the cut-even channel and their disagreement is the cut-odd channel. The disagreement is then used as a predeclared regime coordinate for choosing between two frozen decoders.

The Sanskrit-derived names are mnemonic labels, not claims that standard operator theory has been replaced or historically rediscovered.

\subsection{Dependency architecture rather than four independent discoveries}

The paper preserves source numbers 41--44 for traceability, but does not present them as four unrelated discoveries:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.8em,style=nextline]
\item[Theorem 41.] Foundational cut-graded generator and bilateral-flow theorem.
\item[Theorem 42.] Foundational covariant response-tower theorem.
\item[Theorem 43.] Capstone synthesis of the first two results, adding the observed-flow remainder and first-recognition order.
\item[Theorem 44.] Refinement-ledger theorem and consequences for the typed tail; its elementary closure identities are distinguished from its structural use.
\item[Finite-data results.] Reproducible nuclear and electronic adapters, reported separately from the abstract theorems.
\end{description}

This hierarchy matters. Bilateral conjugacy inherited by Theorem 43 is not counted as a second proof of Theorem 41, and an identity that follows from defining a tail as $F-M_r$ is treated as implementation structure rather than independent physical evidence.

\subsection{Three levels of evidence}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{E\arabic*.},leftmargin=2.7em]
\item \textbf{Formal proof.} Operator identities are proved from stated Hilbert-space, cut, domain, and boundedness hypotheses.
\item \textbf{Exact finite certificate.} Rational fixtures verify theorem wiring, negative controls, and finite-rank consequences without floating-point fitting.
\item \textbf{Empirical validation.} Frozen nuclear and electronic data test adapters, guards, coverage, residuals, subgroup behaviour, and decoder selection.
\end{enumerate}

An exact certificate is not empirical evidence for a physical identification. An empirical improvement is not a proof of an operator theorem. Their value lies in meeting at a typed interface.

\subsection{Novelty boundary}

Several ingredients are classical or standard: involutive gradings, functional calculus for exponentials, Taylor remainders, covariant jets, Lagrange's identity, and Hilbert-space range factorization. The claimed contribution is their fail-closed integration into one target-relative architecture, together with explicit finite certificates and two physically different adapters. Novelty is therefore asserted at the level of the composed recognition interface, theorem governance, and validated adapter design, not by renaming each classical identity.

\subsection{Claim discipline}

The paper does not claim that one physical generator has been derived for all systems, that the algebraic cut is uniquely nuclear, that every residual is memory, or that a cross-fitted historical validation is a future prediction. The source data support a narrower result: a cut-graded recognition architecture has exact finite realizations and guarded adapters whose declared identities and performance metrics are reproducible.

\section{Native notation and admissibility hypotheses}

Let $\Hh$ and $\Yy$ be complex Hilbert spaces. Let
\[
 J=J^*=J^{-1}
\]
be a self-adjoint unitary involution, called the \emph{cut}. Its spectral projections are
\[
 P_\pm=\frac{I\pm J}{2}.
\]
Let $G$ be a bounded operator on $\Hh$ unless an unbounded extension is explicitly invoked. Define
\[
 U_t=e^{tG}.
\]
Let $B:\Hh\to\Yy$ be a bounded observer and let $L:\Hh\to\C$ be a scalar target.

For an unbounded $G$, every displayed identity involving $JGJ$, $e^{tG}$, commutators, or powers of $G$ is licensed only after a common invariant core $\mathcal D$ is specified such that
\[
 J\mathcal D=\mathcal D,
 \qquad
 G\mathcal D\subseteq\mathcal D,
\]
and the required closures or functional calculi exist. This manuscript proves the bounded theory and records the unbounded interface as an open extension unless the stated analytic hypotheses are supplied.

\begin{principle}[Fail-closed ordering]
Algebraic covariance, carrier typing, and recognition-kernel admissibility are checked before a predictive value is promoted. A failed structural gate produces an open-seam or abstention state, not an opportunity to rename the defect after observing the target.
\end{principle}
