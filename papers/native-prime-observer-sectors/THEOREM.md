## POS-T1. Primes classify elementary additive observers

Every transport-stable additive subgroup of Lambda is n Lambda for a
unique integer n>=0. Finite quotients require n>=1. Write

\[
V_n=\Lambda/n\Lambda\simeq(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^2.
\]

For n>=2,

\[
\boxed{V_n\text{ is elementary}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
n\text{ is prime}.}
\tag{1}
\]

Every V_n, including composite n, is transport-stable. A prime selects
an elementary additive quotient, not the only possible stable observer.
There are p^2 elements in V_p.

**Proof.** In adapted coordinates put E=J-I=E_12. Then

\[
ER=E_{11},\quad RE=E_{22},\quad RER=E_{21}.
\tag{2}
\]

Thus the integer operator ring generated by R,J is all M_2(Z).
In native coordinates the same units are

\[
e_{12}=N,\quad e_{11}=NR=(I+K)/2,\quad
e_{22}=RN=(I-K)/2,\quad e_{21}=RNR=R+N.
\tag{3}
\]

In particular the coordinate selectors are the existing seam projectors.
If L is invariant under R,J, its additive closure makes it invariant
under all these units. The coordinate selectors separate the coordinates
of every element of L, and E_12,E_21 exchange them. Let
I_L={a in Z:(a,0) in L}; the same set is obtained in the second coordinate,
and L=I_L x I_L. Every additive subgroup of Z is nZ: if nonzero take its
least positive element n and use division with remainder. Hence L=nZ^2.
Conversely nZ^2 is invariant under all integer matrices and under the
integer inverses of R,J. Conjugating by S proves the native statement.

Subgroups of V_n lift to subgroups of Z^2 containing nZ^2. The invariant
ones are therefore exactly dZ^2/nZ^2=dV_n with d dividing n. For prime n
only d=1,n occur. For composite n a divisor 1<d<n gives a proper nonzero
invariant subgroup. The n=1 quotient is zero and is not simple. QED.

Full generator invariance matters. In adapted coordinates the checkerboard
L={x:x_1+x_2 is even} is invariant under R and diag(1,-1), hence under
the inherited rotation/reflection group. It is not invariant under J:

The quotient must also be intrinsic to Lambda. Since det S=2, reducing

## POS-T2. Local layers, Chinese remainders and exact carries

If n=product_p p^{r_p}, coordinatewise reduction gives an equivariant
additive isomorphism

\[
V_n\simeq\prod_{p\mid n}V_{p^{r_p}}.
\tag{4}
\]

Each primary factor has the full invariant-subgroup chain

\[
V_{p^r}\supset pV_{p^r}\supset\cdots\supset p^rV_{p^r}=0,
\qquad p^kV_{p^r}/p^{k+1}V_{p^r}\simeq V_p.
\tag{5}
\]

The compatible resolution maps V_{p^{r+1}} -> V_{p^r} have fibers of
size p^2. Their inverse limit is Z_p^2 with its componentwise compatible
integer-matrix action. For r>1, (5) is not a direct sum of r copies of
V_p as an additive group or operator module.

**Proof.** For pairwise coprime q_i=p_i^{r_i}, Bezout gives integers
c_i with c_i(n/q_i)=1 modulo q_i. The elements
e_i=c_i(n/q_i) modulo n equal 1 in component i and 0 in every other
component. The inverse to reduction is x=sum_i e_i x_i modulo n,
coordinatewise. Integer matrices commute with these scalar operations,
so the maps intertwine every G action. This proves (4).

T1 classifies the invariant subgroups at n=p^r. The map
[x]_p -> [p^k x] modulo p^{k+1}V_{p^r} is well-defined, onto and has
zero kernel, proving the layer assertion. The inverse-limit description
is the definition of Z_p in each coordinate; continuous transport is
defined componentwise and is compatible with every reduction. Finally
V_{p^r} contains an element of additive order p^r, while every element
in a direct sum of V_p copies is killed by p. This proves nonsplitting
as a full sum of layers. QED.

There is nonetheless a bijective **set** of digits

\[
x=\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}p^j a_j\pmod {p^r},\qquad
a_j\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}^2.
\tag{6}
\]

For an adapted integer g in SL(2,Z), its exact action on those digits is

\[
c_0=0,\quad w_j=g a_j+c_j,\quad
b_j=w_j\bmod p,\quad c_{j+1}=(w_j-b_j)/p.
\tag{7}
\]

Remainders are componentwise in 0,...,p-1; carries may be negative.
Induction gives
g sum_{j<k}p^j a_j=sum_{j<k}p^j b_j+p^k c_k, which proves (7),
including negative entries and the final discarded multiple of p^r.

For p=2,r=2,g=J, a_0=(1,1),a_1=(0,0), the outputs are
b_0=(0,1),b_1=(1,0). Applying J independently modulo 2 to each input
digit would give b_1=(0,0), an incorrect result. Thus the simple layers
are real quotient layers, and their assembly retains inter-layer carries.
This is ordinary exact integer carrying in the chosen lattice; it does
not import the nonassociative historical UGD stored-state operation.

There is a useful scalar invariant recovered by the prime-depth records.
For x=S(a,b) define content c(x)=gcd(a,b)>=0, with c(0)=0. Then

\[
c(gx)=c(x),\qquad
O_{p^k}(x)=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ p^k\mid c(x).
\tag{7a}
\]

For nonzero x this content completely classifies its modular orbit.
Indeed an integral matrix preserves the ideal generated by its coordinates
