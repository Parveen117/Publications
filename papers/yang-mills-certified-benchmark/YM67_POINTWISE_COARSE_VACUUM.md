# YM-67: pointwise coarse vacuum, native derivatives and finite exact pins

Monty Dabas. 4 October 2026. **Python 3.12 only.**

YM66 gives an L1 limit for fixed coarse marginals. Here the native heat
kernel and differentiated vacuum equation give uniform positive density
and derivative bounds. They upgrade that limit to C1 and define
conditional densities at every configuration of finitely many coarse
links. Distant finite pins have an explicit, spatially decaying effect
on both conditional density and its logarithmic derivative.

The per-link logarithmic derivative bound is independent of volume and
product-link length. It also bounds the actual diagonal coarse drift
relative to local form energy. The spatial constants for conditioning
depend on the number of pins; uniform control under arbitrary growing
exterior conditioning and locality of the full Schur memory kernel
remain open.

## Setup and methods

Retain YM66's finite open boxes or periodic cubes, full native compact
link spaces, and Hamiltonian

\[
 A=L_E-V,\qquad V=\sum_p j_pW_p,\qquad |j_p|\le\eta<1/8000.
                                                               \tag{1}
\]

Its normalized positive smooth vacuum h obeys Ah=Eh. The native
derivative D_(e,u) is generated by q_e -> exp(-tu/2)q_e, for a unit
imaginary quaternion u. Every such derivative commutes with L_E.
The order of two derivatives is retained throughout.

Write M=4eta, the uniform bound on the sum of absolute face couplings
incident on one link. Define the following constants:

\[
 \begin{split}
 r_0&=\frac{21}{19}e^{18M},&
 c_0&=e^{9M}\left(\frac1{20}+9M\right)<\frac{11}{200},\\
 B&=\frac{(9/2)M r_0^2e^{9M}}{1-c_0},&
 B_2&=\frac B2+2B^2,\\
 a_1&=2B,& a_2&=B+6B^2,& \ell_2&=B+10B^2,&
 K&=e^{4\pi B}.                                           \tag{2}
 \end{split}
\]

The bound on c_0 holds even at M=1/2000. At eta=0, B=0 and K=1
exactly. No series truncation in eta is used in these definitions.
The quotient B/M increases with M: its positive exponential numerator
increases and its positive denominator decreases. The endpoint enclosure
gives B/M<6, hence the convenient uniform bounds B<=24eta and a_1<=48eta.

Feynman--Kac comparison and positive-semigroup derivative estimates are
established methods; see, for context, [Thompson,
arXiv:1811.03471](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03471).
The proof here uses YM53's explicit native heat bound and the exact
commutation with the native Casimir. It does not import a derivative
estimate with unspecified volume constants. Coarse conditional regularity
requires proof in its own right; [van Enter, Fernandez and Sokal,
hep-lat/9210032](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9210032) gives the broader
distinction between finite marginals and Gibbsian coarse specifications.

## YM67-T1: a pointwise one-link ratio from native heat

Fix a link e and collect in V_e every face term containing it. Then
||V_e||_infinity<=M and
A=L_e+A_rest-V_e, where A_rest is independent of q_e. Set

\[
 g_e(z)=\int h(x,z)\,d\Phi(x)>0.                            \tag{3}
\]

The other links are denoted z. Positivity and Feynman--Kac comparison
give, at time nine,

\[
 e^{-9M}e^{-9(L_e+A_{\rm rest})}h
 \le e^{-9A}h=e^{-9E}h
 \le e^{9M}e^{-9(L_e+A_{\rm rest})}h.                     \tag{4}
\]

YM53's full-content heat estimate is 19/20<=k_9(x,x')<=21/20.
Applying it to (4), with the same positive rest factor at every x,
proves h(x,z)/h(y,z)<=r_0. Averaging y therefore gives

\[
 r_0^{-1}g_e(z)\le h(x,z)\le r_0g_e(z).                    \tag{5}
\]

Integrating the eigenvalue equation over q_e gives
(A_rest-U_e)g_e=E g_e, where
U_e(z)=integral V_e(x,z)h(x,z)dPhi(x)/g_e(z) and |U_e|<=M.
Another bounded-potential comparison gives, for every s>=0,

\[
 e^{sE}e^{-sA_{\rm rest}}g_e\le e^{sM}g_e.                \tag{6}
\]

All these comparisons are pointwise on a fixed finite compact volume.
Their constants do not involve the total number of links. Smoothness
and positivity make every weighted supremum used below finite before
the volume-independent bound is proved.

## YM67-T2: the differentiated vacuum contracts in one link

A face is an ordered product of four unit quaternions, each link
occurring once. Inserting a unit imaginary quaternion from one
derivative gives a factor 1/2; two ordered insertions give 1/4.
Consequently, for any unit native directions i,j, possibly on the same link,

\[
 |D_iV|\le M/2,\qquad |D_iD_jV|\le M/4.                  \tag{7}
\]

Only faces containing all relevant links contribute. These bounds
also follow from the exact one-link identity
4 sum_a (D_(e,a)W_p)^2=1-W_p^2 on the unit carrier.
The antisymmetric part of the second derivative retains [D_i,D_j].

Let Y=D_(e,u)h. It has zero reference average in q_e. Differentiating
Ah=Eh gives

\[
 (L_e+A_{\rm rest}-E)Y=V_eY+(D_{e,u}V)h.                 \tag{8}
\]

Use the Duhamel identity for (8) over a time interval of length nine.
For its initial term, the zero average permits K_9-Pi_e in place
of K_9. The heat-kernel deviation bound gives
||(K_9-Pi_e)f||_infinity<=||f||_infinity/20.
Use (6) on the rest evolution and (5) on the source term. If
N=sup_(x,z)|Y(x,z)|/g_e(z), this yields

\[
 N\le e^{9M}\left(\frac1{20}+9M\right)N
                 +\frac92 e^{9M}M r_0.                 \tag{9}
\]

Solving the strict contraction and then using h>=g_e/r_0 proves

\[
 \boxed{|D_i h|/h=|D_i\log h|\le B.}                     \tag{10}
\]

The estimate holds for every unit direction, so the Euclidean norm
of the three-component log-gradient on one link is at most B.

Now let Y=D_iD_jh, with i on link e. Its average in q_e is again zero,
including when both derivatives act on that same link. Since every
native derivative commutes with the electric Casimir,

\[
 (A-E)Y=(D_iD_jV)h+(D_jV)D_ih+(D_iV)D_jh.               \tag{11}
\]

The right side has magnitude at most M(1/4+B)h. Splitting off V_e
and repeating (9) proves

\[
 \boxed{|D_iD_jh|/h
 \le\frac{9e^{9M}M(1/4+B)r_0^2}{1-c_0}
       =B_2.}                                           \tag{12}
\]

This includes distinct links and arbitrary ordered unit directions.
No symmetry of D_iD_j is presumed. At zero coupling the bounds vanish.
The proof uses the whole heat kernel; no finite representation cutoff
or approximation to h occurs.

## YM67-T3: one pivot per coarse link controls the actual coarse density

Use YM64's disjoint product-link paths, of any fixed integer length b.
For each retained link, write its fine product as x=Pq, where q is
the last fine link. Keep all preceding fine links as hidden coordinates
and replace q by x. This coordinate change preserves product reference.

A coarse derivative x -> exp(-tu/2)x becomes
q -> exp(-t P^(-1)uP/2)q. Conjugation rotates the three native
directions orthogonally. Its coefficients depend only on hidden
coordinates, so further coarse derivatives do not differentiate them.
Different retained paths have different pivots. Thus (10) and (12)
transfer to first and ordered second coarse derivatives of h with
**one pivot per coarse link**, without a factor b.

Let w_Y be the actual vacuum marginal density of m=|Y| coarse links.
Differentiation under its finite-volume reference integral gives

\[
 \boxed{|D_i\log w_Y|\le a_1,\quad
  |D_iD_jw_Y|/w_Y\le a_2,\quad
  |D_iD_j\log w_Y|\le\ell_2.}                            \tag{13}
\]

For the first bound, the score is the conditional average of
2D_i h/h. For the second,
D_iD_j(h^2)/h^2=2D_iD_jh/h+2(D_ih/h)(D_jh/h), whose magnitude
is at most 2B_2+2B^2=a_2. Subtracting the product of first scores
gives the last bound. The first bound also holds for the full
three-component gradient on any one coarse link.

The native metric with these directions orthonormal has diameter
2pi on Q. Moving the m pivots one at a time shows that the oscillation
of log h^2 in those variables is at most 4pi m B. After normalization,
and then averaging over any hidden coordinates, it follows that

\[
 \boxed{K^{-m}\le w_Y(x)\le K^m.}                         \tag{14}
\]

The same bound holds for the density of Y conditioned on any fixed
finite collection Z of other coarse links:

\[
 K^{-|Y|}\le k_{Y|Z}(y|z)
 =\frac{w_{Y\cup Z}(y,z)}{w_Z(z)}\le K^{|Y|}.             \tag{15}
\]

To obtain the constant in (15), condition first on all non-pivot fine
variables. The normalized pivot density obeys (14); the conditional
law given the other coarse products is a mixture of those densities.
Every other coarse product is independent of the chosen pivots because
the paths are disjoint. Smooth positive finite-volume densities make
(15) valid for every z, rather than only almost everywhere.
Target-direction scores and second log derivatives obey (13) as well.

At eta=1/16000, certified bounds include

\[
 B<0.001466662,\quad a_1<0.002933323,\quad
 \ell_2<0.001488173,\quad K<1.018601503.                   \tag{16}
\]

These constants are independent of volume, the number of other links,
and the length b of each disjoint product path.

## YM67-T4: fixed coarse marginals converge in C1 at every configuration

The reference volume of a native ball of radius 0<r<=1 is

\[
 \Phi(B_r)=\frac{r-\sin r}{2\pi}\ge\frac{r^3}{40}.         \tag{17}
\]

This follows from the unit-quaternion polar parameter s/2:
the radial density is sin^2(s/2)/pi. The alternating sine bound gives
r-sin r>=19r^3/120, and 19/(240pi)>1/40.

Consider two m-link densities w,w' arising above, and write
epsilon=||w-w'||_1. Their pointwise difference has oscillation at most
L_m r on a product of m such balls, where

\[
 L_m=4mBK^m.
\]

Average the inequality at a maximum of |w-w'| over this product ball.
For every radius r in (0,1],

\[
 \|w-w'\|_\infty\le
 U_m(\epsilon;r):=\min\{2K^m,\;40^m\epsilon/r^{3m}+L_mr\}.
                                                               \tag{18}
\]

For fixed eta>0 and m, optimizing r gives
O(epsilon^(1/(3m+1))) as epsilon tends to zero. At eta=0 every
normalized density is exactly one. The certificate uses explicit
dyadic radii, so its finite bounds need no numerical root finder.

YM66 provides epsilon<=E_X(R;eta) for a distant boundary removal,
where X consists of all fine paths used by these m links and
|X|=bm. Equation (18) therefore proves uniform convergence of w_Y.
Its limit is strictly positive by (14).

There is a quantitative derivative upgrade. If
delta=||w-w'||_infinity and f=log w-log w', then
||f||_infinity<=K^m delta and |D_i^2 f|<=2ell_2.
The central difference along a native flow gives, for 0<t<=1,

\[
 \boxed{\|D_i\log w-D_i\log w'\|_\infty
  \le\min\{2a_1,\;K^m\delta/t+\ell_2t\}.}                \tag{19}
\]

This is O(sqrt(delta)) for small delta. Uniform convergence of the
functions and these derivatives proves a C1 limit. The common second
derivative bounds also give locally Lipschitz first derivatives in
native charts. An infinite-volume second derivative need only exist
almost everywhere; no C2 convergence is claimed.

For fixed finite Y,Z, (14) keeps the denominator in (15) uniformly
positive. Hence their conditional densities and target scores converge
uniformly at every exact z. For example, if delta is the sup-norm
error in the joint density, marginalization bounds the denominator
error by delta and

\[
 \|k_{Y|Z}-k'_{Y|Z}\|_\infty
 \le K^{|Z|}(1+K^{|Y|})\delta.                           \tag{20}
\]

The target score difference is controlled directly by (19) for the
joint density, since the denominator has no target variable.
This constructs the canonical continuous conditional version at
points of zero probability. It uses positivity and regularity in
addition to YM66's L1 convergence.

## YM67-T5: distant finite exact pins have small density and score effects

Let Y and Z be disjoint finite coarse sets whose complete fine paths
are separated by link-graph distance d>=1. Write m=|Y|+|Z| and
S=min(b|Y|,b|Z|). YM65 bounds every covariance of bounded norm-one
readouts on these supports by

\[
 \delta_d=\min\left\{1,
 \left(1+\frac{2S}{3}+\frac1{3\rho_*d}\right)e^{-\rho_*d}
 \right\},                                               \tag{21}
\]

with rho_* from YM66. At eta=0 take delta_d=0.
This alone would not allow division by an event of vanishing probability.
The new bounds (13)--(15) supply the missing density control.

Put G(y,z)=w_(Y union Z)(y,z)-w_Y(y)w_Z(z). On a product of
reference balls its integral is a covariance of two indicator functions,
so its magnitude is at most delta_d. Both terms in G have the derivative
bound used in (18). Averaging on the product ball therefore gives
||G||_infinity<=U_m(delta_d;r), for every allowed r. Denote any such
bound, or its infimum over r, by U. Dividing by the density floor gives

\[
 \begin{split}
 \sup_{y,z}|k_{Y|Z}(y|z)-w_Y(y)|&\le K^{|Z|}U,\\
 \sup_{y,z}\left|\log\frac{k_{Y|Z}(y|z)}{w_Y(y)}\right|
                                  &\le K^mU.             \tag{22}
 \end{split}
\]

These statements hold uniformly in the exact pinned values z, in
finite volumes and in the unique infinite-volume limit. Applying the
same central difference to the log ratio gives, for a unit target
direction i and 0<t<=1,

\[
 \boxed{\sup_{y,z}|D_i\log k_{Y|Z}(y|z)-D_i\log w_Y(y)|
       \le\min\{2a_1,\;K^mU/t+\ell_2t\}.}                \tag{23}
\]

For fixed sets and fixed b, (22) decays exponentially with a possible
polynomial prefactor and rate rho_*/(3m+1); (23) has rate
rho_*/(2(3m+1)). The constants depend on m. Two different pinned
values can be compared by summing their bounds against w_Y.

The classical mutual information of the two coarse sets is also bounded:
write the joint density relative to w_Y w_Z as 1+u. Then |u|<=K^mU,
and log(1+u)<=u gives relative entropy at most chi-square divergence.
Thus

\[
 0\le I(Y:Z)\le(K^mU)^2.                                 \tag{24}
\]

One may additionally cap the log ratio and I by
2|Y|log K=8pi|Y|B, using (14)--(15). This is a statistical
information bound, not a formula for a physical mass.

For eta=1/16000, two coarse plaquette supports (four links each),
and b=2, upward-rounded bounds from explicit radii are:

| Fine support separation d | Conditional density change | Target score change | Mutual information |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 256 | 0.058551 | 0.005867 | 0.003973 |
| 512 | 0.007319 | 0.005867 | 0.00006208 |
| 1024 | 0.00011445 | 0.00086483 | 0.00000001518 |

These control the whole pinned coarse configurations, including their
gauge-invariant loop readouts. They are conservative error bounds,
not sampled correlations. The long-distance arithmetic uses directed
120-place rounding after a bounded-argument exponential, avoiding an
absolute 30-place rounding floor on very small decays.

## YM67-T6: the actual diagonal coarse drift is uniformly controlled

At finite volume let w_b be the full retained vacuum density. YM64's
diagonal coarse generator on smooth functions is

\[
 C_b=bL_{\rm coarse}
        -b\sum_{e,a}(D_{e,a}\log w_b)D_{e,a}.             \tag{25}
\]

For f depending on a finite set Y of retained links, (13) and
Cauchy--Schwarz give the concrete local operator estimate

\[
 \boxed{\|(C_b-bL_{\rm coarse})f\|_{L^2(\mu_b)}
       \le a_1\sqrt{b|Y|}\,C_b[f]^{1/2}.}                \tag{26}
\]

Here C_b[f]=b integral Gamma_coarse(f)dmu_b. After dividing the
generator by b, the coefficient is independent of b:
a_1 sqrt(|Y|) times the square root of C_b[f]/b. It is also
independent of volume for fixed Y, and a_1 tends to zero with eta.
The full density can still depend on every retained coordinate;
(26) controls the drift size without presuming that dependence is local.

For a fixed marginal Y, its diagonal generator uses w_(b,Y).
T4 now supplies uniform convergence of its drift coefficients.
T5 controls how those coefficients change when a fixed finite distant
set is pinned exactly. These are actual vacuum-density statements,
with no fitted one-parameter potential substituted.

The remaining spatial problem is stronger: compare conditionals under
arbitrary exterior configurations agreeing near the target, with bounds
independent of the number of pinned links, and propagate that control
through the unbounded Schur coupling and hidden inverse. The factors
in (18)--(23) do not provide that uniform exterior estimate.
No full spatial memory-kernel theorem, time-zero Markov identification,
or four-dimensional continuum construction is asserted here.
