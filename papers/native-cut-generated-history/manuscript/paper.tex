\begin{abstract}
We give a complete representation and verification procedure for information
transport in a declared finite cut/operator framework. Successive binary
projective cuts generate an isometric coherent history, retaining the full
input operator algebra on a known image subspace. A positive quadratic form
of rank $r$ has a minimum $r$-channel weighted amplitude record, with exact
descent of form-preserving linear operations. We construct $n^2$ calibrated
quadratic readings at rational circular observer positions that reconstruct
every Hermitian observable on $n$ complex channels. This count is minimal
for fixed scalar readings of an unrestricted Hermitian observable. The same
family gives a necessary-and-sufficient finite certificate for all-input
linear invariant transport, an explicit decoder for positive metrics, and
a deterministic reconstruction-error bound. An inherited two-channel
thermodynamic form shows exactly how a circular probe detects information
missed by axis readings after classical branch extraction. The manuscript
completes this finite mathematical verification problem and integrates
standard dilation, square completion and polarization in one native exact
implementation. The resulting interface is available for quantum-gravity
models; it does not by itself select gravitational dynamics, quantize local
constraints, or establish physical faster-than-light communication.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem and the completed contribution}

An invariant value, a recoverable information record and a complete
verification procedure are different objects. Two sources can have the same
quadratic amount while differing in phase or in their responses to a later
cut. A successful transport claim must specify which information is retained,
which operations are allowed, and which observations can distinguish a
correct transport from an incorrect one.

The native research programme formulates these questions through cuts,
retained records and comparison-dependent observables. Its quantum-gravity
motivation is to relate operational information to geometry without treating
an uncalibrated coordinate or operation count as an absolute physical clock.
Here we settle a finite mathematical component of that programme: complete
quadratic-observer verification of coherent information transport.

The completed chain is
\begin{equation}
\text{declared cuts}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{coherent record}\
\longrightarrow\ \text{complete observer certificate}.
\end{equation}
Each arrow has an explicit construction and proof. Assumptions fix the
meaning of verification; the identity being tested is not among those
assumptions. Circular observer geometry provides a separating family of
readings, not a circular logical justification of the conclusion.

The ingredients have established mathematical precedents. Coherent
measurement dilation and recovery on an isometry's image are standard
\cite{watrous}. Weighted square completion and polarization are standard
linear algebra. Informationally complete measurements are a developed
subject \cite{scott}; restrictions such as known low rank alter the relevant
reconstruction problem \cite{baldwin}. Our contribution is a source-bound
integration of these ingredients in the native cut calculus, including an
explicit rational observer family, minimum-count proof, transport test,
decoder and reproducible exact certificates. We do not claim tomography or
measurement dilation as new physical discoveries.

\section{Observation contract and notation}

Let $V$ be an $n$-dimensional positive complex carrier with specified adjoint
and calibrated coordinates. Executable examples use the exact field
$\mathbb Q+\ii\mathbb Q$, with $\ii^2=-1$, represented by the unchanged native
cut-scalar engine. General statements are finite-dimensional statements over
$\mathbb C$. The carrier is an admitted representation of the cut algebra;
this paper does not derive it, physical spacetime, or all historical UGD
arithmetic from an empty set of premises.

A cut is an orthogonal projection $P$, with complement $Q=\Id-P$. The
selected projective implementation has branch operators $P,Q$. A cut
predicate alone does not uniquely select a physical instrument. Likewise,
the ordered cut schedule is protocol data; no gravitational field equation
is inferred merely from its existence.

A quadratic observable is $H\in\Herm(n)$ with calibrated reading
\begin{equation}\label{eq:q}
\qform{H}{x}=x^\adj Hx.
\end{equation}
Hermiticity, quadratic dependence, dimension and calibration are explicit
hypotheses. Distinct probe preparations or reproducible expectation values
are permitted. The observations below are not simultaneous exact
measurements of a single unknown quantum specimen. In a statistical
implementation each expectation must be estimated, and uncertainty enters
the error bound in Section~\ref{sec:error}.

At a stipulated uncut level the accessible algebra may be only
$\mathbb C\Id$. Every complex-linear unital map acts identically there,
since $T(a\Id)=a\Id$. This is zero observable distinction within that
algebra, not zero geometric dimension or a proof that inaccessible change
cannot occur. A nontrivial cut instead generates
$\{aP+bQ\}\simeq\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C$. Repeating the same ideal cut is
idempotent and does not by itself create a clock.

\section{Coherent records and the minimum amplitude representation}

For a finite ordered cut tree of depth $k$, let $E_{j,h,b}$ denote the
projection for outcome $b\in\{0,1\}$ at step $j$, after prefix $h$.
Each node obeys $\sum_b E_{j,h,b}^\adj E_{j,h,b}=\Id$. For a history
$h=(h_1,\ldots,h_k)$, define the ordered branch product $K_h$ and record
\begin{equation}\label{eq:history}
W_kx=\sum_{h\in\{0,1\}^k}|h\rangle\otimes K_hx.
\end{equation}
The record includes relative branch phases and the final carrier, not just
the outcome labels.

\begin{proposition}[Complete coherent history]\label{thm:history}
For every finite declared cut tree, $W_k^\adj W_k=\Id$. If
$R_k=W_kW_k^\adj$, the map $J_k(O)=W_kOW_k^\adj$ is an injective
$*$-isomorphism onto the corner $R_k\operatorname{End}(\mathrm{record})R_k$.
It has inverse $B\mapsto W_k^\adj BW_k$ on that corner.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The empty history has $K_\emptyset=\Id$. Appending a cut to a prefix gives
\[
\sum_b K_h^\adj E_{j,h,b}^\adj E_{j,h,b}K_h=K_h^\adj K_h.
\]
Induction yields $\sum_h K_h^\adj K_h=\Id$, proving the isometry.
Inserting $W_k^\adj W_k=\Id$ gives
$J_k(AB)=J_k(A)J_k(B)$ and $W_k^\adj J_k(O)W_k=O$; adjoints are preserved.
Every corner operator $B=R_kBR_k$ equals
$J_k(W_k^\adj BW_k)$. The corner identity is $R_k$, not the identity of
the larger record carrier.
\end{proof}

No pre-cut Hamiltonian, continuous time or exponential is needed for this
construction. A later embedding into continuous dynamics is a separate
contract. On amplitudes the decoder $W_k^\adj$ has norm one. On densities,
$W_k^\adj(W_k\rho W_k^\adj)W_k=\rho$.

Classicalizing the history produces $\bigoplus_h K_h\rho K_h^\adj$ and
removes cross-history blocks. For a single cut the lost part is
$P\rho Q+Q\rho P$. If two inputs have equal first-cut diagonal blocks,
every later classical history beginning with that cut is identical for
them. The coherent records can nevertheless remain distinct.

\begin{proposition}[Minimum weighted amplitude record]\label{thm:factor}
Let $H\geq0$ have rank $r$ and entries in $\mathbb Q+\ii\mathbb Q$. There
are a full-row-rank $r\times n$ matrix $L$ and a positive rational diagonal
matrix $D$ such that $H=L^\adj DL$. Exactly $r$ linear scalar amplitude
channels are necessary and sufficient for this positive weighted
representation. The record $Lx$ is faithful on $V/\ker H$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a nonzero positive residual, choose a positive diagonal pivot $a$ and
divide its row by $a$ to obtain $\ell$. Subtraction of
$a\ell^\adj\ell$ leaves the positive Schur complement and reduces rank
by one. A positive matrix with every diagonal zero is zero, since each
two-coordinate positive restriction has zero off-diagonal entry. Exact
iteration gives $r$ independent rows and positive rational pivots.
Conversely, a sum of $m$ positive weighted rank-one forms has rank at most
$m$. Finally, positivity gives $\ker L=\ker H$.
\end{proof}

The nonnegative summands $D_{jj}|(Lx)_j|^2$ admit a binary Pythagorean
synthesis with $r-1$ merges and minimum possible maximum depth
$\lceil\log_2 r\rceil$ for $r\geq1$. These are synthesis counts, not
classical bit lengths. Retaining only the summands or their sum loses
phase; retaining the linear amplitudes preserves the quotient record.

If $T^\adj HT=H$, kernel invariance gives a unique descended operator $U$
with $LT=UL$ and $U^\adj DU=D$. To construct it choose
$S=L^\adj(LL^\adj)^{-1}$ and set $U=LTS$. Since $LS=\Id$ and
$T\ker L\subseteq\ker L$, this intertwining identity follows directly.
This formula applies for $r>0$; the zero form has the empty quotient record.
Additional operations that do not preserve $H$ can require a larger record;
the previously proved operation-closure criterion computes the span of
rows $LT_w$ over allowed finite words \cite{crc,chd}.

\section{Complete circular-observer verification}

Choose $a=3/5$, $b=4/5$ and standard coordinate vectors $e_i$. The observer
family is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probes}
\mathcal P_n=\{e_i\}_i\ \cup\
\{ae_i+be_j,\ ae_i+\ii be_j: i<j\}.
\end{equation}
It contains $n^2$ vectors. Every vector obeys $p^\adj p=1$, and $pp^\adj$
is an orthogonal binary cut. The two-coordinate vectors lie on circles in
the real planes spanned by $(e_i,e_j)$ or $(e_i,\ii e_j)$. The projectors
are separately accessible tests and are not asserted to sum to $\Id$ or
form a symmetric informationally complete POVM.

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\begin{theorem}[Complete observer and transport certificate]\label{thm:main}
Under the declared finite quadratic observation contract:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The readings $\{\qform{H}{p}:p\in\mathcal P_n\}$ reconstruct every
$H\in\Herm(n)$. The count $n^2$ is minimum among fixed scalar quadratic
readings for an unrestricted Hermitian observable.
\item For any specified linear $T:\mathbb C^n\to\mathbb C^m$ and specified
Hermitian $G_{\rm in},G_{\rm out}$, equality of the input and output form
readings on $\mathcal P_n$ is equivalent to
$T^\adj G_{\rm out}T=G_{\rm in}$ and hence to equality on every input.
If both metrics are positive definite, an exact left decoder is
$G_{\rm in}^{-1}T^\adj G_{\rm out}$.
\item For every coherent cut history $W$, the transported observers $Wp$
reconstruct the entire image-corner observable $WHW^\adj$. More generally,
they certify $W^\adj BW=H$ for a specified output observable $B$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
d_i=\qform{H}{e_i},\quad
r_{ij}=\qform{H}{ae_i+be_j},\quad
s_{ij}=\qform{H}{ae_i+\ii be_j}.
\]
For $H_{ij}=u_{ij}+\ii v_{ij}$, direct expansion gives
\begin{align}
25r_{ij}&=9d_i+16d_j+24u_{ij},\\
25s_{ij}&=9d_i+16d_j-24v_{ij}.
\end{align}
Therefore the exact inverse is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse}
\begin{aligned}
H_{ii}&=d_i,\\
\operatorname{Re}H_{ij}&=\frac{25r_{ij}-9d_i-16d_j}{24},\\
\operatorname{Im}H_{ij}&=\frac{9d_i+16d_j-25s_{ij}}{24}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $H_{ji}=\overline{H_{ij}}$. It reconstructs a Hermitian matrix from
every real reading vector and substituting it back recovers that vector.

The real dimension of $\Herm(n)$ is $n+2\binom n2=n^2$. Any fixed $m$
scalar quadratic readings define a real-linear map to $\mathbb R^m$;
when $m<n^2$, its kernel is nonzero. Thus it cannot separate all observables.
Even positivity alone does not remove the obstruction: a nonzero invisible
Hermitian direction $A$ makes $c\Id$ and $c\Id+\epsilon A$ positive with
identical readings for sufficiently small nonzero $\epsilon$ and $c>0$.

For transport, the probe differences are the readings of the Hermitian
defect $D_T=T^\adj G_{\rm out}T-G_{\rm in}$. The inverse above proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iff}
\bigl[\qform{D_T}{p}=0\quad\forall p\in\mathcal P_n\bigr]
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D_T=0.
\end{equation}
If the metrics are positive, $Tx=0$ implies $x^\adj G_{\rm in}x=0$ and
therefore $x=0$. Multiplication verifies the stated left decoder.

Finally, Proposition~\ref{thm:history} gives
\[
(Wp)^\adj(WHW^\adj)(Wp)=p^\adj Hp.
\]
Reconstruction first gives $H$ and then $WHW^\adj$. Applying the same
argument to $W^\adj BW-H$ proves the compression test.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a finite iff test for a general all-input identity,
not a fit to the displayed source example. Removing any one probe produces
an explicit invisible direction: apply~\eqref{eq:inverse} to a reading
vector with one at the removed position and zero elsewhere. The native
certificate constructs these witnesses.

If the observable is known real symmetric, the imaginary probes are
unnecessary and the minimum is $n(n+1)/2$. If its trace is known, one axis
reading follows from the trace and the other diagonal readings, leaving
$n^2-1$ independent scalar data for an arbitrary trace-fixed Hermitian
matrix. Neither count is a universal minimum for pure states, adaptive
protocols or arbitrary nonlinear encodings.

On a known history image corner, the count depends on the original $n$,
not the enlarged history dimension. It does not identify arbitrary
off-image blocks of an output operator. Likewise, reconstructing a Gram
record $xx^\adj$ fixes a nonzero $x$ only up to one common unit phase.
It is not recovery of that phase from classical quadratic readings.

\section{Frame covariance, error control and composition}\label{sec:error}

For an invertible calibrated frame change $S$, transform
\begin{equation}
p'=Sp,\qquad H'=S^{-\adj}HS^{-1},\qquad
G'_{\rm ref}=S^{-\adj}S^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Then $p'^\adj G'_{\rm ref}p'=1$ and $p'^\adj H'p'=p^\adj Hp$.
The probe family remains circular relative to the transported reference
metric. A nonunitary coordinate transformation need not preserve its
Euclidean coordinate norm. This common-frame covariance does not license
independently unknown calibration changes for different readings.

\begin{proposition}[Deterministic error bound]
Suppose every scalar reading in~\eqref{eq:probes} has absolute error at
most $\epsilon$. The reconstructed Hermitian observable satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:error}
\|\widehat H-H\|_{\rm op}\leq
\left(1+\frac{25(n-1)}6\right)\epsilon.
\end{equation}
The same coefficient bounds an all-input transport defect when every exact
probe defect is bounded by $\epsilon$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:inverse} bounds each diagonal error by $\epsilon$ and
each real or imaginary off-diagonal error by
$(25+9+16)\epsilon/24=25\epsilon/12$. Thus each complex off-diagonal
magnitude is at most $25\epsilon/6$. The maximum absolute row sum is
bounded by the right side of~\eqref{eq:error}. For a Hermitian matrix,
apply the eigenvector equation at a component of maximum magnitude to
bound every eigenvalue by that row sum. For a transport defect, conclude
$|x^\adj D_Tx|\leq\|D_T\|_{\rm op}\|x\|^2$.
\end{proof}

Calibration error and statistical uncertainty must be included in the
reading budget. Small estimated defects do not prove an exactly zero
identity. The bound is deterministic and conservative; no optimal sampling
claim is made.

Certificates also compose. If $T_1^\adj G_1T_1=G_0$ and
$T_2^\adj G_2T_2=G_1$, substitution proves
$(T_2T_1)^\adj G_2(T_2T_1)=G_0$. No return to the initial point or external
time parameter is required. For semidefinite forms, recovery remains a
quotient statement as in Proposition~\ref{thm:factor}.

L=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D=\diag(2,9/2),\qquad H=L^\adj DL.
\end{equation}
Two independent amplitude channels are necessary and sufficient. One
binary Pythagorean merge evaluates the quadratic total.

\section{What the certificate implies for geometry}

The construction supplies a rigorous interface for a proposed
information--geometry model: specify its carrier, accessible observables,
cuts, retained record and transport; then apply the complete family to its
claimed invariant identities. A nonzero reconstructed defect identifies
exactly which quadratic information fails to match. This is stronger than
checking one radius or a few axis values.

Norm preservation alone does not select curvature. For example, let
\begin{equation}
U=\begin{pmatrix}3/5&-4/5\\4/5&3/5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
V=\diag(1,\ii),\qquad K=V^\adj U^\adj VU.
\end{equation}
Both $\Id$ and this nontrivial loop preserve every Euclidean norm, whereas
$K^\adj P K-P\neq0$. The complete observer family detects the changed
cut observable. Interpreting an algebraic loop as physical spacetime
curvature requires an additional geometric and dynamical identification.
Also, the nonlinear map $x\mapsto\|x\|e_1$ preserves norms while erasing
direction. The linearity hypothesis in the decoder theorem is substantive.

The existing programme has separate geometric and physical results. Its
finite hinge and matter--geometry packets provide selected geometric
realizations and joint interactions; the local coframe construction has
classical constraint closure in a specified $1+1$ model \cite{record,lnc}.
None of these changes the hypotheses of the present theorem. In particular,
classical constraint closure is not a proof of quantum anomaly cancellation
or of a positive physical sector in $3+1$ dimensions.

The signalling audit is similarly separate \cite{gsa}. Local
trace-preserving quantum operations give zero remote contrast. The inherited
nonlinear branch-sensitive extension yields a conditional finite contrast
$1/10$, but requires an additional physical state-law assumption and has
no calibrated faster-than-light arrival comparison. Circular verification
does not discharge those obligations. No gravitational experiment or
physical transmission-speed measurement is reported here.

The completed mathematical result therefore closes this manuscript's
representation and verification problem. To close a physical quantum-gravity
construction would additionally require a justified matter/geometry law,
its physical quantum constraints and domains, appropriate limiting behaviour,
and calibrated distinguishing observations. These are separate research
claims rather than omitted steps in the finite reconstruction proof.

\section{Conclusion}

We have completed a finite operational verification problem: declared binary
cuts construct a recoverable coherent history; a positive quadratic source
has a minimum weighted amplitude record; and a minimum calibrated family
of circular observer positions reconstructs every quadratic observable and
certifies its linear transport on all inputs. The construction includes an
explicit decoder, common-frame covariance, a deterministic error bound and
an exact source example that exposes information missed by axis-only checks.

Within this scope, the observation contract determines what can be verified,
and the separating observer family proves the claimed invariant identity
without assuming it. The completed interface can now be used to test a
specified quantum-gravity model's information-transport claims. Selecting
and validating that physical model remains distinct from the mathematical
completion established here.
