# NM1: native algebra, modular lifts and information seen by an observer

## Contract and conventions

Work in the existing unital associative EMK sector over Q (or its scalar
extension to R), with R^2=-I, K^2=I and KR=-RK. This is not the historical
UGD digit-carry operation; UGD-A0 already proves that its strict stored-state
addition is not associative. Let L=RK. Products act on column vectors from
the left; AB means apply B, then A. Claims about general words below have
written proofs; finite exact executions test their implementations.

The frozen EMK-1 representation is

\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
K=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
L=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The preliminary discussion used diagonal K. The following rational adapter
makes that change explicit, without changing the native source.

## NM1-T1 — a modular subgroup with a stated lattice

The algebra generated by R,K has basis I,K,R,L and is isomorphic to M_2(Q).
The multiplicative subgroup generated by R,K alone has just the eight elements
{I,-I,R,-R,K,-K,L,-L}. Define

\[
N=(L-R)/2,\quad T=I+N,\quad S=I+R,\quad S^{-1}=(I-R)/2.
\]

Then N is nonzero, N^2=0, T^n=I+nN for every integer n, and

\[
R^2=(RT)^3=-I,\qquad R^4=I.
\]

In the adapted coordinates,

\[
S^{-1}RS=R,\quad S^{-1}KS=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1),\quad
S^{-1}TS=U=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Choose the lattice Lambda=S Z^2, with ordered basis (1,1),(-1,1).
Then <R,T> = S SL(2,Z) S^{-1}, exactly the determinant-one automorphisms
of Lambda. In native coordinates
T=[[1/2,1/2],[-1/2,3/2]]; it does NOT preserve the ambient Z^2.

**Proof.** Move K past R using KR=-RK and reduce squares; every word is
plus or minus one of I,R,K,L. These eight matrices are distinct and closed.
The four unsigned matrices are linearly independent and span all 2x2 rational
matrices, proving the algebra statement. L^2=I and LR+RL=0 give N^2=0.
Thus (I+N)(I-N)=I and induction, including negative n, proves the power law.
Direct multiplication with S and S^{-1} gives the adapted matrices. There,
(RU)^2=RU-I, so (RU)^3=-I. Conjugate back for the native relation.

For completeness, R,U generate SL(2,Z) rather than merely a subgroup satisfying
those relations. For A=[[a,b],[c,d]] of determinant 1 and c nonzero, choose
q with r=a-qc and 0<=r<|c|. Left multiplication by R U^{-q} replaces the
first column by (-c,r). Its bottom entry decreases in absolute value.
Iterate the Euclidean algorithm until c=0. Determinant 1 then implies
a=d=epsilon in {1,-1}, so the remaining matrix is epsilon U^{epsilon b}.
Every reduction step is invertible using R,U, and -I=R^2 is also generated.
This proves generation. Conjugation proves the lattice statement. Finally,
T(1,0)=(1/2,-1/2) is not integral, exhibiting the lattice distinction. QED.

The rational scalar 1/2, additive combination and chosen integral lattice
are load-bearing. T is not a word in R,K alone. The algebra permits this
adapter; it does not canonically select it. No complex analytic space or
physical clock has been assumed in this theorem.

## NM1-T2 — exactly one sign coordinate, with an unavoidable correction

Use adapted coordinates G=SL(2,Z) and quotient Q=G/{I,-I}. Write pi for
this quotient. For each class g choose s(g) whose first nonzero entry in
row-major order is positive; s(e)=I. Define the sign sigma(g,h) by

\[
s(g)s(h)=\sigma(g,h)s(gh),\quad \sigma(g,h)\in\{1,-1\}.
\]

The sign is normalized and satisfies

\[
\sigma(g,h)\sigma(gh,k)=\sigma(h,k)\sigma(g,hk).
\]

Consequently the exact lift law is

\[
(\epsilon,g)(\eta,h)=(\epsilon\eta\sigma(g,h),gh).
\]

The map (epsilon,g) -> epsilon s(g) is a group isomorphism onto G.
Each projective endpoint has exactly two lifts, so distinguishing the full
matrix endpoint needs at least two labels per fiber and this construction
attains the bound. This is a two-valued algebraic coordinate, not a claim
about entropy for a supplied probability distribution.

**Proof.** A determinant-one matrix is nonzero, so exactly one of A,-A
satisfies the section rule. Compare (s(g)s(h))s(k) and s(g)(s(h)s(k)).
Associativity and centrality of signs give the displayed identity. It proves
associativity of the lift; the map is multiplicative and bijective by construction.
Two distinct matrices A,-A cannot have the same label in an injective recovery,
which proves the fiberwise lower bound. QED.

No section of pi can be a group homomorphism. Indeed, g=pi(R) has order 2,
while both its possible lifts R and -R square to -I, not I. A homomorphic
section would have to send g to an element squaring to I, a contradiction.
In particular sigma(g,g)=-1 for every normalized section. Changing section
to s'(g)=b(g)s(g), b(e)=1, only changes the correction to
sigma'(g,h)=b(g)b(h)b(gh)^{-1}sigma(g,h); it cannot remove it globally.
This is the elementary nonsplit central extension SL(2,Z) -> PSL(2,Z),
not a newly discovered general theory of cocycles.

## NM1-T3 — loss that survives continuation, and loss that does not

For the projective observer pi, A and -A remain indistinguishable after
any common left and right continuations B,C in G:

\[
\pi(BAC)=\pi(B(-A)C).
\]

They are also indistinguishable by the conjugation observer X -> AXA^{-1}.
Conversely, if A and B induce the same conjugation on every X in M_2(Q),
then B^{-1}A commutes with every matrix. Commuting with the matrix units
forces it to be scalar; determinant 1 makes that scalar plus or minus 1.
Thus the full conjugation observer on G has exactly the same two-point fibers.

**Proof of persistence.** Multiplication propagates a central sign unchanged,
and it cancels in conjugation. This is a congruence of the group, so a fixed
observer using only these operations cannot recover the discarded sign. QED.

A scalar trace observer has different behavior. The two operators U and U^{-1}
both have trace 2. After the SAME left continuation B=RU,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(BU)=2,\qquad \operatorname{tr}(BU^{-1})=0.
\]

Thus trace equality is not stable under continuation. The sign-invariant
readout (tr A)^2 also distinguishes them afterwards (4 versus 0). These
operators were already different in PSL(2,Z); this is not recovery of a
central sign. The same examples transfer to the native lattice by S.

Neither the sign ledger nor a matrix endpoint recovers the entire operation
history. For the declared quarter-turn history n in Z, the endpoint is R^n,
which identifies n and n+4. The projective endpoint also identifies n and n+2.
A full n register distinguishes these histories; one sign coordinate does not.
For arbitrary mixed words, an integer count alone is not claimed sufficient.
An exponent history is not automatically a continuous path or its winding:
those require their own path contract, as in the existing EMK-1 winding result.

No data destruction in invertible transport follows from these quotients.
Also, a nontrivial cocycle for choosing representatives is not, without a
specified connection and transport contract, EMK-C1 connection curvature.

## NM1-T4 — how the sign enters a modular transformation law

Only here introduce the classical upper half plane H and the adapter
A.z=(az+b)/(cz+d), with j(A,z)=cz+d. For real determinant-one A,
Im(A.z)=Im(z)/|cz+d|^2>0, so all expressions are defined on H. Direct
substitution gives

\[
j(AB,z)=j(A,B.z)j(B,z).
\]

The kernel of the action on all H is {I,-I}: an operator acting identically
satisfies cz^2+(d-a)z-b=0 for all z, so c=b=0, a=d and a^2=1.
For the section from T2 this same identity becomes

\[
j(s(g),s(h).z)j(s(h),z)=\sigma(g,h)j(s(gh),z).
\]

Therefore j(s(g),z)^k has the ordinary composition law on Q for even integer
k, while for odd integer k it retains the factor sigma(g,h). Nonsplitting
in T2 prevents removing that factor by merely changing matrix representatives.

If a scalar function satisfies f(A.z)=j(A,z)^k f(z) for every A in G,
then A=-I gives f(z)=(-1)^k f(z). For odd k the only such function is zero.
This requires neither holomorphy nor a Fourier expansion. With an additional
scalar multiplier chi, a nonzero function instead requires
chi(-I)(-1)^k=1, in addition to all other multiplier compatibility conditions.
This necessary central condition alone does not construct such a function.

These are standard integer-weight modular identities instantiated in the
native adapter. Half-integral weights require additional branch/multiplier
structure. The two-valued matrix lift here is not asserted to supply it.

## NM1-T5 — completion has a functional defect and a uniqueness gate

Let V be a specified vector space of functions on H stable under the integer
weight right action

\[
(f|_k A)(z)=j(A,z)^{-k}f(A.z).
\]

T4 gives (f|A)|B=f|(AB). For a given f in V define D_A=f|A-f. Then

\[
D_{AB}=D_A|B+D_B.
\]

A correction C in V makes F=f+C invariant precisely when
C|A-C=-D_A for every A. If one solution C0 exists, every solution is
C0+H with H invariant in V, and every such sum is a solution.

**Proof.** Expand the right action on f, subtract f, and insert f|B.
Expand invariance of f+C to obtain the correction equation. Subtract the
equations of two corrections; their difference is invariant. Conversely an
invariant addition does not change the equation. QED.

Without restrictions this is not a mock-modular construction: C=-f always
produces the uninformative invariant F=0. At weight zero, f(z)=z has distinct
corrections -z and 1-z, completing it to 0 and 1 respectively. Both obey the
same defect equation. Specifying a nonzero target, function space, analytic
regularity, growth, normalization and (where appropriate) a shadow is essential.
A smaller allowed correction class may have no solution at all.

A nonholomorphic correction for a concrete mock theta series, its shadow and
half-integral-weight multiplier are not derived by this sign calculation.
The next native theorem must choose a concrete history function and prove its
transformation law and admissible correction, rather than assign an existing
mock theta function the name 'lost memory'.

## Evidence and limits

The certificate replays the native source matrices, lattice adapter, exact
integer generation, section cocycle, counterexamples and automorphy factors.
