A. S. Ustunel and M. Zakai, *The change of variables formula on Wiener
space*, Seminaire de probabilites (Strasbourg) 31 (1997), 24-39:
https://www.numdam.org/article/SPS_1997__31__24_0.pdf

The accessible primary PDF supplies the Gaussian divergence and
Carleman--Fredholm conventions in section II, equations (2.2)-(2.5),
the change-of-variables density in section III, especially (3.9)-(3.10),
and the bijective H-C1 version in Theorem 3.1. In our notation its
pullback density is det_2(I+Du) exp[-delta u-||u||_H^2/2]. Ramer's
original result is J. Functional Analysis 15 (1974), 166-187; we do not
claim to have retrieved that original paper in this pass. IGQ's
previous primary-source criterion remains frozen.

Application checks: the fixed-alpha shape map is bijective with the
inverse from g^-1; u is H-valued, H-C1 with Hilbert--Schmidt derivative
and invertible I+Du. The finite angle map contributes its ordinary
Jacobian. RGR-T1 computes the conditional Fourier trace and det_2
factor; RGR-T2 checks the stochastic contraction in L2 before combining
the factors. No infinite-dimensional Lebesgue measure is invoked.

The conditional trace uses paired sine/cosine cutoffs, not an arbitrary
summation order. The singular second measure is integrated only against
pointwise continuous tests; continuous density versions are explicitly
derived. The rank-one pairing is a selected physical prescription.
Its one-channel quotient does not classify the full solution dual.

Finite witnesses: the fourth-derivative chain rule and integration by
parts; regularized logarithmic determinant cancellation; quadratic
energy cocycle; finite Fourier trace at the identity; exact Gaussian
Ward moments for a divergence-free nonlinear model; positive rank-one
quotient and observable intertwiners. The nonlinear finite model is an
analogy checking signs and covariance factors, not a Fourier truncation
of the diffeomorphism group. No finite test proves Ramer's theorem,
Gaussian singularity, Frechet completeness or the continuum determinant.
