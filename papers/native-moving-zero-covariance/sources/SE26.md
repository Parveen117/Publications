## 2. Theorem 1: zero contrast is a whole diagonal

Let V be one admitted native module and compare two readings on V.
For E(a,b)=(b,a), define

\[
 C(a,b)=a-b,\qquad P_\pm=(I\pm E)/2.
 \tag{SE26.1}
\]

Then E^2=I, E^dagger=E, and P_+ and P_- are complementary
self-adjoint idempotents. Moreover

\[
 \ker C=\operatorname{ran}P_+
     =\Delta(V):=\{(a,a):a\in V\},\qquad
 C^\dagger C=2P_-.
 \tag{SE26.2}
\]

In a one-dimensional real reading chart, Delta(V) is the entire
diagonal line, not just the origin. In the full module it is a copy
of V. Writing m=(a+b)/2 and d=(a-b)/2 gives

\[
 (a,b)=(m,m)+(d,-d),\qquad
 \|a\|^2+\|b\|^2=2\|m\|^2+2\|d\|^2.
 \tag{SE26.3}
\]

**Proof.** Expand E^2 and (I+/-E)^2; their cross product is zero.
The pairing gives C^dagger h=(h,-h), hence C^dagger C=I-E.
The kernel statement and the mean/contrast reconstruction follow
directly. Orthogonality of the two summands proves the norm identity.
All calculations use the native field's characteristic-zero arithmetic.
For a faithful unitary chart U, transport by U direct-sum U commutes
with E and takes the diagonal to the transported diagonal. QED.

The zero contrast d=0 does not assert a=b=0: the common coordinate m
remains. Discarding m loses the entire diagonal submodule. A diagonal
cut, its zero-valued output and the field's zero scalar therefore have
distinct roles. This theorem does not claim that zero ceases to be a
number or that a picture alone supplies a lawful TVSP-to-VTSP arrow map.

## 4. Theorem 3: a lossless circle representation of the source

Let A=A^dagger be a bounded source operator on the admitted positive
native completion, and choose a positive scalar a. With the derived
iota^2=-1 and iota^dagger=-iota, put

\[
 R_a=(aI-\iota A)^{-1},\qquad
 U_a=(aI+\iota A)(aI-\iota A)^{-1}.
 \tag{SE26.7}
\]

Then U_a is unitary, I+U_a is invertible, and

\[
 A=-\iota a(U_a-I)(U_a+I)^{-1},\qquad
 \ker A=\ker(U_a-I),
 \tag{SE26.8}
\]
\[
 \frac{U_a-U_a^\dagger}{2\iota}
       =2aR_a^\dagger A R_a.
 \tag{SE26.9}
\]

In particular A>=0 if and only if the left side of (SE26.9) is
nonnegative. An invertible congruence preserves every negative
subspace dimension and nullity. The source sign has a faithful circle
form; merely having unit radius is not its positivity condition.

**Proof.** The positive pairing gives
||(aI-iota A)h||^2=a^2||h||^2+||Ah||^2. Hence this operator is
bounded below, has closed range, and its adjoint has trivial kernel.
Its range is therefore also dense, so it is onto and invertible.
The inverse commutes with A. Multiplication gives U_a^dagger U_a=I
and I+U_a=2aR_a. These identities give (SE26.8). Also
R_a-R_a^dagger=2iota R_a^dagger A R_a, which proves (SE26.9).
Since R_a is a bounded bijection, positivity and negative subspaces
transport in both directions. QED.

For a real scalar t this is the rational circle coordinate

\[
 u_a(t)=\frac{a^2-t^2}{a^2+t^2}
          +\iota\frac{2at}{a^2+t^2},\qquad |u_a(t)|=1.
 \tag{SE26.10}
\]

It is one-to-one from the real line to the circle without the point
-1, with inverse t=a Im(u)/(1+Re(u)). Zero maps to the marked identity
point 1; the positive and negative half-lines map to distinct oriented
semicircles. No logarithmic branch or phase deletion is needed. The
circle changes the representation while retaining the measured phase.
This is the classical Cayley rational transform, explicitly reproved on
the already admitted native carrier; no novelty priority is claimed.

Apply the theorem to A=S_b for the actual eta-zero target, or to
A=S_b+eta I_X for a shifted comparison. Transporting a positive shifted
operator does not turn it into the unshifted source.

\[

**Proof.** Expand S_tilde+delta I_X and retain both terms. As a
