# LCF-1 — Cut-induced coframe density, matter source and local covariance

We derive a positive spatial coframe density from a nondegenerate cut-gradient, extend the inherited matter generator by that density, and prove that its transformation cancels the residual. The same coupling yields an explicit variational matter source for a spatially varying circle cut. The normal–normal bracket still needs a geometry-sector normal evolution; we compute that missing term rather than declare full closure.

This is a local continuum identity on smooth fields, with exact native polynomial/word certificates. No physical spatial coordinate, gravitational action or quantum representation of local geometry is claimed to follow uniquely from the cut alone. In this packet `x` is a declared spatial interpretation of one control direction, and `s` remains an operation parameter. The earlier global quantum AGD generator is not silently replaced by a quantum field theory of `theta(x)`.

## 1. A spatial coframe from the existing relational metric

Let `P(x)` be a smooth rank-r orthogonal cut in a finite positive carrier, with anti-Hermitian reference connection `A_x` and `X=D_x P=partial_x P+[A_x,P]`. Define, where positive,

\[
 W_{cut}=\frac1{2r}\operatorname{Tr}(X^2),\qquad
 e_{cut}=\sqrt{W_{cut}}>0. \tag{1}
\]

This is RCG's metric coefficient, not a new metric theorem. It is invariant under simultaneous native unit-frame changes of the cut and reference connection. Under an infinitesimal spatial relabelling generated by `f(x)`, take
`delta_f P=f P'`, `delta_f A_x=f A_x'+f'A_x`. Product differentiation gives

\[
 \delta_f X=fX'+f'X,\quad
 \delta_f W_{cut}=fW_{cut}'+2f'W_{cut},\quad
 \boxed{\delta_f e_{cut}=(f e_{cut})'.} \tag{2}
\]

Thus `e_cut dx` is a spatial coframe density in this declared coordinate representation. A scalar overlap alone transforms as weight zero and cannot be assigned (2) without an additional rule; its **spatial derivative metric** supplies the missing density weight.

For the native circle cut

\[
 P(\theta)=\tfrac12(I+C\cos\theta+S\sin\theta),\quad
 C=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1),\quad S=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]

in the parallel reference chart, `Tr(P_theta^2)=1/2`, whence

\[
 W_{cut}=\frac{(\theta')^2}{4},\qquad
 e_{cut}=\frac{|\theta'|}{2}. \tag{3}
\]

On a patch with `theta'>0`, `e_cut=theta'/2` and its variation is ordinary differentiation. A constant cut has `W_cut=0`; it does not define a nondegenerate coframe by (1). We do not repair that degeneracy by inserting a nonzero scale without declaring it. A zero of `theta'` needs a different patch/model; absolute-value differentiation across it is not used. In particular AGD's single global circle coordinate has no such spatial gradient: the composite coframe is an additional local cut-field sector, not a derivation of AGD's fixed background coframe from its global mode. Its quantum completion and classical recovery limits must be established separately.

Circle phase alone does not recover a global lift or its sheet record. UGD-G1/EMK-G3 remain relevant to that global bookkeeping. This packet is on one oriented regular patch and does not identify a continuous angle with a finite UGD phase alphabet or erase seam ledgers.

## 2. The extended inherited matter operator

Work on compactly supported smooth two-component spatial half-densities. Set

\[
 D_f=f\partial_x+\tfrac12 f',\quad
 \boxed{A_N(e)=C D_N+gR N e},\qquad
 R=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}, \tag{4}
\]

where `f,N,e,g` are real, `g` is constant and `e>0`. These are formal anti-Hermitian operators on this core; essential self-adjointness of a new time-dependent field model is not asserted. At `e=1` the operator is exactly AGD's local diagnostic generator. The derivative and half-density terms are unchanged; the rest factor now carries the spatial coframe scale.

If one adopts the inherited GCP coframe adapter, on a patch with nonzero `alpha=N e` write

\[
 e^0=\alpha\,ds,\qquad e^1=e(dx+\beta ds),\qquad
 q=-\alpha^2ds^2+e^2(dx+\beta ds)^2. \tag{5}
\]

The principal speeds are `-beta +/- N`, so the null directions agree with (5). At `e=1`, `beta=0`, `N=v`, this recovers GCP/AGD's `q=-v^2 ds^2+dx^2` and rest coefficient `gv`. Thus the comparison dictionary agrees at the inherited specialization; it is not a claim that the principal symbol uniquely fixes a metric scale or all time-dependent spin-connection terms.

For the generator contract (4), the transformation laws are

\[
 \delta_f e=(fe)',\quad \delta_f N=fN'-Nf',\quad
 \delta_f\alpha=f\alpha'. \tag{6}
\]

The last identity follows by varying `alpha=Ne`. The coframe interpretation and the lapse density weight therefore agree.

## 3. Exact cancellation of the mixed residual

Let `b(f,N)=fN'-Nf'`. Direct multiplication on the core gives

\[
 [D_f,D_h]=D_{b(f,h)},\qquad
 [A_N(e),A_M(e)]=D_{b(N,M)}, \tag{7}
\]
\[
 \boxed{[D_f,A_N(e)]=A_{b(f,N)}(e)+gRN(fe)'.} \tag{8}
\]

For (7), `C^2=I` supplies the derivative bracket. In the normal–normal bracket the mass cross terms are proportional to
`N(Me)'+N'Me-M(Ne)'-M'Ne=0`, and the two multiplication mass terms commute. For (8), the mass commutator is `gR f(Ne)'`; subtracting the mass part `gR(fN'-Nf')e` leaves `gRN(fe)'`. At `e=1` this is precisely AGD's rejected `gRNf'` term.

Now the field variation of the same operator is

\[
 \delta_e A_N[(fe)']=gRN(fe)'.
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{[D_f,A_N(e)]-\delta_e A_N[(fe)']=A_{b(f,N)}(e).} \tag{9}
\]

This repairs the specified mixed local covariance law. It does not cancel the term by setting `g=0` or by declaring the coframe frozen under a transformation that changes it.

A diagnostic weight-w rule `delta e=f e'+w f'e` would leave `(1-w)gRNef'` in (9). Therefore weight one is required for this cancellation when the coupling is nonzero and general local transformations are allowed. The cut-gradient construction independently supplies exactly that weight.

## 4. Enlarged generator algebra and the remaining obstruction

On smooth test functionals of `e` (or formal local jet expressions), define the field-direction derivation

\[
 V_f F[e]=\left.\frac{d}{dt}F[e+t(fe)']\right|_{t=0},
 \qquad \mathcal D_f=D_f-V_f,\qquad \mathcal A_N=A_N(e). \tag{10}
\]

`D_f` acts on the spatial half-density argument, while `V_f` differentiates field dependence. Hold smearing labels fixed under field differentiation. They commute as operations on test sections with independent `x` and field arguments. The vector-field identity is

\[
 [V_f,V_h]e=(h(fe)'-f(he)')'=-V_{b(f,h)}e.
\]

The same holds on the admitted functionals by derivation. Combining it with (7)–(9),

\[
 [\mathcal D_f,\mathcal D_h]=\mathcal D_{b(f,h)},\qquad
 [\mathcal D_f,\mathcal A_N]=\mathcal A_{b(f,N)}, \tag{11}
\]
\[
 \boxed{[\mathcal A_N,\mathcal A_M]
 =\mathcal D_{b(N,M)}+V_{b(N,M)}.} \tag{12}
\]

The first two brackets close. The last one still lacks geometry-field normal transport, because `mathcal A_N` acts only on the matter argument and does not change `e`. For example `N=1`, `M=x`, `e=1+x^2` gives `b=1` and `V_b e=2x`, nonzero. Equivalently, acting on a test section containing `e(x)`, the proposed full spatial generator has an extra field derivative absent from the normal commutator.

Thus the old **mixed** obstruction is resolved in this extended contract, while the precise remaining **normal** obligation is displayed. This is not a first-class gravitational constraint algebra, and the field derivative in (12) must not be quotiented away if the coframe is an observable dynamical sector.

## 5. Matter action covariance and the derived cut source

With real controls `N,e,beta`, consider the inherited linear matter action

\[
 S_m=\int ds\,dx\; i\eta\,\psi^*
 \big(\partial_s-A_N(e)-D_\beta\big)\psi,\qquad \eta>0. \tag{13}
\]

Boundary conditions remove integration-by-parts terms. Its equation is the linear `partial_s psi=(A_N(e)+D_beta)psi` at prescribed fields. This is the same evolution convention as AGD's `i eta partial_s psi=H psi`, `H=i eta(A_N+D_beta)`.

For time-dependent `f(s,x)`, use

\[
 \delta\psi=D_f\psi,\quad
 \delta e=(fe)',\quad \delta N=b(f,N),\quad
 \delta\beta=\partial_s f+b(f,\beta). \tag{14}
\]

Let `B=A_N+D_beta`. Equations (8) and (14) give
`delta B=partial_s D_f+[D_f,B]`. Hence the differential operator `L=partial_s-B` has `delta L=[D_f,L]`, so `delta(L psi)=D_f(L psi)`. Since `D_f` is anti-Hermitian in the spatial pairing, the integrated variation of (13) vanishes. This proves spatial relabelling covariance of the action, including time-dependent labels; it is not full normal-deformation invariance.

Define the real matter bilinear

\[
 J=i\eta g\,\psi^*R\psi,\qquad
 \frac{\delta H_m}{\delta e}=N J,\qquad
 \frac{\delta S_m}{\delta e}=-N J. \tag{15}
\]

Reality follows from `(iR)^*=iR`. At fixed `psi`, changing `e` changes only `gRN e` in (4), which proves (15). Under (14), `delta J=fJ'+f'J`; `NJ` is a scalar, so the source pairing `NJ delta e dx` has the required covariance.

Use the **cut-derived** coframe on the oriented patch in (3), keeping the independent control `N` fixed during this variation. Then

\[
 \delta H_m=\tfrac12\int NJ\,\partial_x\delta\theta\,dx,
 \qquad
 \boxed{\frac{\delta H_m}{\delta\theta}
       =-\tfrac12\partial_x(NJ).} \tag{16}
\]

If a local canonical cut-field action with momentum `p_theta` and a separately specified geometry Hamiltonian `H_g` is admitted, the **matter contribution** to its momentum equation is therefore

\[
 \left.\partial_s p_\theta\right|_{matter}
 =+\tfrac12\partial_x(NJ). \tag{17}
\]

This source is derived from the inherited rest coupling and the cut-gradient metric, not fitted or inferred just from the size of an invariant. The derivative in (17) makes the spatial structure essential. With constant `NJ` there is no local bulk cut force; boundary forces require their own boundary conditions.

The variation contract matters: holding `alpha=Ne` fixed rather than `N` makes the rest term `alpha J` independent of `e`, but the kinetic factor `N=alpha/e` then varies. One must vary that kinetic term as well; (16) is explicitly the fixed-N result and is not a universal fixed-lapse formula.

A canonical classical complex-field realization or a background-field quantum matter action can use this variation. Neither constitutes the missing quantum theory of a local cut field. Feeding a state-dependent classical field back into an unknown one-particle state is not asserted to be an autonomous linear quantum channel; QGC's ensemble-consistency obligation remains.

## 6. Exact source and algebra controls

The polynomial certificate evaluates general coefficient identities, not a numerical finite-difference derivative. It uses exact nonconstant `e`, lapse and shift polynomials, checks both differential orders, verifies the required weight and the normal obstruction, and checks time-dependent action covariance at the operator level.

For a local source example at `eta=1`, take
`psi(x)=(1, i x)^T`, `g=2/3`, `N=1+x`, with a smooth compact cutoff equal to one on the displayed patch. Then

\[
 J=\frac43x,\qquad
 \tfrac12(NJ)'=\frac23+\frac43x. \tag{18}
\]

These are local polynomial source densities; the uncut polynomial is not called a normalized state on the whole line. For any compactly supported variation within the patch, (16) follows by exact integration by parts. Rational coefficient fixtures separately verify this identity with variations vanishing at the ends of a finite test interval.

In the circle metric examples, `theta'=2` gives `W_cut=e_cut^2=1`, while `theta'=0` is degenerate. The rational-angle native cuts reproduce (3) without evaluating trigonometric functions numerically. The old metric and recognition curvature remain distinct targets; adding a coframe transformation does not identify them.
